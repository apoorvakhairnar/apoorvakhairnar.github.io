**The Goal:** Study and present the numerical methods used in a published paper on modeling and optimizing surface textures for tilting pad thrust bearings, which reduce power loss in rotating shafts.

**My Role:** Analyzed and presented advanced numerical modeling techniques from published tribology research, translating complex mathematics into a clear technical presentation.

**Project Summary:**
- The model solves the steady-state, mass-conserving Reynolds equation for incompressible fluids using a modified finite-volume method on a non-uniform mesh, to accurately resolve textured surface features.
- The bearing equations are solved with an initial Newton-Raphson iteration followed by Broyden's quasi-Newton method, which converges faster once a good initial guess is available.
- Results were validated against CFD simulations, and simulating an untextured bearing first — then using that solution as the initial guess for the textured case — proved significantly faster than solving from scratch.

**Skills Developed:** Numerical Methods, Newton-Raphson Method, Finite Volume Method, Technical Research & Presentation
