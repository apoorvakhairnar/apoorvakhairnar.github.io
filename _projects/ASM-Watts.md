**The Goal:** Analyze and synthesize Watt's Linkage — a four-bar mechanism invented by James Watt that constrains a point to travel along a near-straight line, widely used in vehicle and rail suspensions.

**My Role:** Led kinematic analysis and physical prototyping of a four-bar mechanism within a four-person team, validating theoretical motion predictions against a working scale model.

**Project Summary:**
- Watt's Linkage is used in place of the Panhard rod in rear-axle car suspensions, keeping the axle centered while resisting sideways motion, and in similar train suspension setups.
- Applied Freudenstein's equation and graphical synthesis to size the mechanism's three bars — two equal-length outer bars and a shorter middle bar — for the desired straight-line path.
- Verified the analysis with velocity and acceleration diagrams (relative velocity method), then validated it with a cardboard-and-styrofoam prototype.

**Skills Developed:** Mechanism Kinematics, Freudenstein's Method, Graphical Synthesis, Physical Prototyping, Technical Collaboration
